- Toxic industrial chemicals (TIC), sometimes named toxic industrial materials (TIM), are chemicals that are widely manufactured, stored, transported and used. These chemicals can be in gaseous, liquid or solid state.
- There are two systems of coding and labeling toxic industrial chemicals. For the transport of chemicals, the codes used are ADR (for road transport), RID (for rail
- transport) or ICAO
- (for air transport). For storage there is system known as CLP, which is
- abbreviation for Classification, Labelling and Packaging Regulation

	- Due to the large quantity of different chemicals and their different qualities, it was hard to tell which one is most or least dangerous to health. To keep toxic industrial chemicals in order, the International Task Force-25 introduced in 1996 a rank called Hazard Index (HI).
	- It consists of four factors:
	- Toxicity
	- State of matter
	- Distribution
	- Producers
- Each factor is assigned a number between 1 and 5. The maximum value of the hazard index is 625. Hazard Index divides toxic industrial
- chemicals into three classes:
  High hazard - hazard index 81 and greater - chemicals that have high toxicity and are widely produced, stored or transported
  Medium hazard - hazard index between 36 and 80 - indicates toxic industrial chemicals which may rank high in some categories but lower in others such as producers, physical state, or toxicity
  Low hazard - hazard index below 36 - indicates that this chemical is not likely to be hazardous unless specific operational factors indicate otherwise.
